Key Findings, Pipeline Learnings & Insights

Part 1 Insights: Data Acquisition, Exploration & Preprocessing
  • Dominance of Invoice Monetary Value: Correlation analysis demonstrated that invoice dollar value (Dollars) is the primary determinant of shipping charges (Pearson r = 0.985), even exceeding physical unit quantity (Quantity, r = 0.947). In carrier logistics, liability insurance, ad-valorem cargo valuation, and bonded shipping surcharges create a strong value-proportional cost baseline.
  • Quantified Economies of Scale: Exploratory volume quartile analysis revealed a non-linear volume discount structure: low-volume orders (≤ 56 units) incurred a median freight cost of $0.10 / unit, whereas bulk shipments (≥ 1,008 units) cost only $0.04 / unit. This represents an empirical ~60% per-unit freight reduction, proving that linear assumptions alone cannot capture tiered logistics pricing contracts.
  • IQR Outlier Capping: Raw freight costs exhibited high positive right-skewness (skewness ≈ 3.8). Applying the 1.5×IQR capping boundary effectively shielded model gradient updates from extreme enterprise transactions without discarding genuine high-volume orders.
  • Domain Feature Engineering: Deriving days_po_to_invoice (turnaround lead time) and Price_per_Unit provided the models with critical density signals, allowing them to differentiate compact high-value shipments from bulky low-margin commodities.
Part 2 Insights: Multi-Model Regression Architecture Evaluation
  • Strong Linear Baseline Performance: Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Linear Regression delivered an unexpectedly strong result (RMSE = $23.70, R² = 0.9863). The strong linear correlation between invoice dollars and freight provides a solid performance floor that simplistic non-linear models struggle to beat.
  • Decision Tree Discretization Failure: A single Decision Tree Regressor underperformed noticeably (RMSE = $30.45, R² = 0.9774). Orthogonal axis-aligned splits partition continuous financial amounts into discrete step-functions, introducing high variance and piecewise prediction jumps on unseen holdout test samples.
  • Ensemble Superiority: Both Random Forest (bagging) and Gradient Boosting (boosting) successfully overcame single-tree variance. Random Forest reduced error variance by averaging across randomized decorrelated decision trees, while Gradient Boosting systematically corrected residual errors on intermediate price brackets.
Part 3 Insights: Hyperparameter Optimization via GridSearchCV
  • Mitigating Overfitting: The default Random Forest regressor with unconstrained depth achieved an RMSE of $25.13 due to leaf-level memorization of supplier noise. Performing GridSearchCV with 5-fold cross-validation revealed that constraining tree depth to max_depth=6 and setting min_samples_split=5 produced the project's best test score: RMSE = $23.46 and R² = 0.9866.
  • Ensemble Parsimony: An ensemble of 50 constrained trees outperformed deeper 100-tree models, demonstrating that in tabular data with 4 well-engineered features, shallower trees prevent variance accumulation while fully capturing non-linear bulk thresholds.
  • Cross-Validated Generalization: 5-fold cross-validation ensured that hyperparameter tuning was immune to holdout test set anomalies, guaranteeing high predictive stability across diverse supplier categories.
Part 4 Insights: Streamlit Web Deployment & Operational Business ROI
  • Bridging Data Science to Operational Workflows: Deploying the models into an interactive web dashboard at https://adsfa2-machine-learning.streamlit.app/ transforms static academic models into an actionable tool for procurement agents and finance managers.
  • Multi-Model Consensus Transparency: Presenting predictions from all 5 models side-by-side provides procurement teams with a clear confidence band, dispelling black-box skepticism and validating estimates against algorithmic consensus.
  • Automated Invoice Auditing & Margin Protection: In commercial retail logistics, carrier overbilling represents a persistent source of margin leakage. Incorporating automated freight sanity thresholds (>15% deviation from expected freight proportion) empowers finance departments to flag and dispute incorrect vendor invoices before disbursement.
  • Production Pipeline Consistency: Serializing both the model and the exact fitted StandardScaler guarantees zero training-serving skew, ensuring that live inference calculations match training conditions precisely.
